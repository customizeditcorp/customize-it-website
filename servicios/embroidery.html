Embroidery vs. screen printing — which is right for you?

Both methods apply your logo or design to garments, but they work differently and suit different needs.

Embroidery — Thread stitched directly into the fabric. More durable, never fades or cracks, and gives a premium textured look. Best for hats, polos, jackets, and workwear where longevity matters.
Screen printing — Ink applied to the garment surface. Better for large coverage designs on t-shirts where color fills a wide area. Lower cost per unit at higher quantities.
DTF (Direct to Film) — A newer print method that bonds a full-color graphic to the garment. Good for photographic designs or complex artwork with many colors on soft goods.

What is the difference between embroidery and screen printing?
Embroidery stitches your design directly into the fabric using thread — it is more durable than screen printing, does not fade or crack, and gives a premium textured look ideal for hats, polos, jackets, and workwear. Screen printing applies ink to the surface and works better for large-coverage designs on t-shirts where color fills a wide area. We offer both and can recommend the right method for your project.
